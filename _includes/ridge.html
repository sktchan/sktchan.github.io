Usage: {% include ridge.html items=list %} Each item needs `name`, `region`, `gain` (metres) and `distance` (km). ─── why the geometry is computed here ────────────────────────────────────── The community ribbon's bar positions come from a generator script because Liquid can't do date arithmetic. Nothing here needs dates — normalising three integers against the largest of them is arithmetic Liquid *can* do — so the ridge is built at render time and stays correct the moment favourites.yml changes. Same principle, less machinery. ─── why there is no JavaScript ───────────────────────────────────────────── ribbon.js exists almost entirely to flip popovers that would run off the frame. With a fixed handful of peaks that decision is knowable at build time: the first peak's popover left-aligns, the last one's right-aligns, everything between is centred. So the reveal is a plain CSS sibling selector and the ridge works with JS disabled. ─── what is data and what is decoration ──────────────────────────────────── Summit heights are real. The saddles between them, the drop to the baseline at either end, and the haze gradient in the fill are not — they are what turns three points into a landform. The SVG is aria-hidden and carries no text: every fact in it is also in a button's aria-label, which is what a screen reader gets. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign items = include.items -%} {%- assign n = items.size -%} {%- comment -%} The viewBox is one 100-unit slice per hike, so the shape is independent of how many there are. `max` is the tallest gain; every summit is a fraction of it. Summits span y=80 (tallest) down to y=18 above the baseline, which keeps even the shortest hike clear of the saddles at y=88 — a peak that dipped below its own saddles would read as a valley. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign vbw = n | times: 100 -%} {%- assign max = items | map: 'gain' | sort | last -%} {%- capture ridgepath -%} M 0,100 {%- for item in items -%} {%- assign ratio = item.gain | times: 1.0 | divided_by: max -%} {%- assign h = ratio | times: 62 | plus: 18 -%} {%- assign y = 100 | minus: h | round: 1 -%} {%- unless forloop.first -%} {%- assign saddle = forloop.index0 | times: 100 %} L {{ saddle }},88 {%- endunless -%} {%- assign px = forloop.index0 | times: 100 | plus: 50 %} L {{ px }},{{ y }} {%- endfor %} L {{ vbw }},100 Z {%- endcapture -%}

That stretches the coordinate system non-uniformly, which would smear the ridge stroke; vector-effect pins it back to a true 2px. Because the stretch is linear, a summit at y=52.6 in the viewBox sits at 52.6% of the plot's height, which is what lets the markers below be positioned in plain percentages. {%- endcomment -%}
