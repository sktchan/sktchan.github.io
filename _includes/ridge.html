{%- comment -%} Favourite hikes as one mountain range: a summit per hike, its height the trail's real elevation gain. Usage: {% include ridge.html items=list %} Each item needs `name`, `region`, `gain` (metres) and `distance` (km). ─── why the geometry is computed here ────────────────────────────────────── The community ribbon's bar positions come from a generator script because Liquid can't do date arithmetic. Nothing here needs dates — normalising a few integers against the largest of them is arithmetic Liquid *can* do — so the range is built at render time and stays correct the moment favourites.yml changes. Same principle, less machinery. ─── why there is no JavaScript ───────────────────────────────────────────── ribbon.js exists almost entirely to flip popovers that would run off the frame. With a fixed handful of peaks that decision is knowable at build time: the first peak's popover left-aligns, the last one's right-aligns, everything between is centred, and each opens away from the edge it is nearest. So the reveal is a plain CSS sibling selector and the range works with JS disabled. ─── what is data and what is drawing ─────────────────────────────────────── ONLY the summit heights are data. Everything that makes these read as mountains rather than as a line chart is drawing: · flanks break at a shoulder instead of running straight to the valley, and the two shoulders sit at different fractions of their flank, so no peak is symmetric — real ones aren't, and symmetry is most of what made the first version look like a graph · the right flank of each peak is shaded, as if the light came from the left. A flat triangle reads as a triangle; a lit one reads as rock · snowcaps, with a ragged snowline, because nothing says "mountain" faster · the saddles between peaks and the drop to the baseline at either end are fixed depths — they say nothing about the walks None of it moves a summit. Change a `gain` and only the heights change. The SVG is aria-hidden and carries no text: every fact in it is also in a button's aria-label, which is what a screen reader gets. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign items = include.items -%} {%- assign n = items.size -%} {%- comment -%} The viewBox is one 100-unit slice per hike, so the shape is independent of how many there are. `max` is the tallest gain; every summit is a fraction of it. Summits span y=80 (tallest) down to y=18 above the baseline, which keeps even the shortest hike clear of the saddles at y=88 — a peak that dipped below its own saddles would read as a valley. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign vbw = n | times: 100 -%} {%- assign max = items | map: 'gain' | sort | last -%} {%- assign ridgepath = 'M 0,100' -%} {%- assign shadows = '' -%} {%- assign caps = '' -%} {%- for item in items -%} {%- assign x0 = forloop.index0 | times: 100 -%} {%- assign x1 = x0 | plus: 100 -%} {%- assign sx = x0 | plus: 50 -%} {%- assign sy = item.gain | times: 1.0 | divided_by: max | times: 62 | plus: 18 -%} {%- assign sy = 100 | minus: sy -%} {%- comment -%} The outermost flanks run to the floor; the inner ones meet at a saddle. {%- endcomment -%} {%- if forloop.first %}{% assign vy0 = 100 %}{% else %}{% assign vy0 = 88 %}{% endif -%} {%- if forloop.last %}{% assign vy1 = 100 %}{% else %}{% assign vy1 = 88 %}{% endif -%} {%- comment -%} The shoulders. Left: 55% of the way across for only 40% of the climb, so the lower slope is the gentle one and the summit end is steep. Right: 42% across for 68% of the drop, so that side falls away fast and then eases. Different fractions on purpose — matching them would restore the symmetry this is here to break. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign lsx = x0 | plus: 27.5 -%} {%- assign lsy = vy0 | minus: sy | times: 0.40 -%} {%- assign lsy = vy0 | minus: lsy -%} {%- assign rsx = sx | plus: 21 -%} {%- assign rsy = vy1 | minus: sy | times: 0.68 | plus: sy -%} {%- capture seg -%} L {{ lsx | round: 1 }},{{ lsy | round: 1 }} L {{ sx }},{{ sy | round: 1 }} L {{ rsx | round: 1 }},{{ rsy | round: 1 }} {%- endcapture -%} {%- unless forloop.first -%} {%- capture saddle %} L {{ x0 }},88{% endcapture -%} {%- assign ridgepath = ridgepath | append: saddle -%} {%- endunless -%} {%- assign ridgepath = ridgepath | append: seg -%} {%- comment -%} Shading. The lit/shadow line drops vertically from the summit, so the wedge is bounded by that line, the right flank, and the floor — all of it inside the silhouette, which is why it needs no clip path. It is filled with a gradient that fades out well before the floor, and that is not decoration for its own sake: at a flat opacity the wedge's vertical edge carried on down through the valley, where there is no flank to justify it, and each mountain read as a bar in a chart. Fading it kills the edge exactly where the slope stops being a slope. {%- endcomment -%} {%- capture shadow -%} {%- endcapture -%} {%- assign shadows = shadows | append: shadow -%} {%- comment -%} The snowcap runs down each flank to just under a third of the peak's height, and the snowline zigzags rather than cutting straight across. Both cap corners are interpolated along the real flanks, so the cap sits inside the mountain however tall it is. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign ph = vy0 | plus: vy1 | times: 0.5 | minus: sy -%} {%- assign capd = ph | times: 0.30 -%} {%- assign capy = sy | plus: capd -%} {%- assign dl = lsy | minus: sy -%} {%- assign clx = lsx | minus: sx | times: capd | divided_by: dl | plus: sx -%} {%- assign dr = rsy | minus: sy -%} {%- assign crx = rsx | minus: sx | times: capd | divided_by: dr | plus: sx -%} {%- assign span = clx | minus: crx -%} {%- assign z1x = span | times: 0.28 | plus: crx -%} {%- assign z2x = span | times: 0.55 | plus: crx -%} {%- assign z3x = span | times: 0.78 | plus: crx -%} {%- capture cap -%} {%- endcapture -%} {%- assign caps = caps | append: cap -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- capture tail %} L {{ vbw }},100 Z{% endcapture -%} {%- assign ridgepath = ridgepath | append: tail | strip_newlines -%}
{%- comment -%} preserveAspectRatio="none" so the range is exactly as tall as the CSS says however wide the column gets — a compact band can't have its height set by the viewport. That stretches the coordinate system non-uniformly, which would smear the ridge stroke; vector-effect pins it back to a true 2px. Because the stretch is linear, a summit at y=52.6 in the viewBox sits at 52.6% of the plot's height, which is what lets the markers below be positioned in plain percentages. {%- endcomment -%}
